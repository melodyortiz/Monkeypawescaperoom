The Monkey's Paw

Three men may have three wishes from it. Watch the story, escape the cottage, and decide for yourself whether fate can be bargained with.

Introduction

It is a cold, wet night at Laburnum Villa. Mr. White and his son Herbert are playing chess while Mrs. White knits by the fire. Then a visitor arrives: Sergeant-Major Morris, home after twenty-one years in India, carrying a small, dried-up paw.

"It had a spell put on it by an old fakir… He wanted to show that fate ruled people's lives, and that those who interfered with it did so to their sorrow."Sergeant-Major Morris, Part I

If you could make three wishes, what would you ask for? And what might it cost you?

Your Task

You will watch a film version of "The Monkey's Paw," learn key vocabulary, and then play an escape room that tests what you know about the plot, characters, and literary devices. To finish, you will write a fourth wish: a new short scene in which someone finds the paw and makes a wish of their own.

The Process

Step 1

Watch the story

Video not playing? Open it on YouTube or try the animated adaptation.

While you watch, answer these on your paper or in your notebook:

  1. Describe the setting in Part I. How does the weather set the mood?
  2. What warning does Sergeant-Major Morris give about the paw? Why does he throw it into the fire?
  3. What is the first wish, and how does it come true?
  4. Who pushes for the second wish? Why?
  5. What do you think is knocking at the door? Use evidence to explain.
  6. Why does Mr. White make the third wish so quickly?
Step 2

Learn the vocabulary

fakir
a holy man in India who lives by begging and is believed to have special powers
credulity
a readiness to believe things too easily
maligned
spoken badly about; unfairly criticized
compensation
money paid to make up for a loss or injury
oppressive
heavy and hard to bear, like a gloomy silence
apathy
a lack of interest or feeling
prosaic
ordinary; dull and everyday
foreshadowing
hints in a story about what will happen later
situational irony
when what happens is the opposite of what was expected
Step 3

Play the escape room

Scroll down to The Escape Room. Each wish is a locked room. Answer every question in a room correctly to earn its key word. Unlock all three, then put the story in order to escape. Write down your three key words and your final escape code.

Step 4

Write the fourth wish

Imagine the paw is found years later by someone new. Write a scene of 250–400 words in which this person makes a wish. Your scene must:

  • Include at least one example of foreshadowing.
  • End with situational irony: the wish comes true in a way the character did not expect.
  • Use at least three vocabulary words from Step 2.
  • Show the story's theme: interfering with fate brings sorrow.

The Escape Room

You are locked inside Laburnum Villa. Something is knocking at the door. Three wishes stand between you and the empty road outside.

Conclusion

The Whites got exactly what they asked for, and it destroyed them. W. W. Jacobs shows that wishes can't change fate without a price. Think about it one more time: if the paw were sitting on your table right now, would you pick it up?
