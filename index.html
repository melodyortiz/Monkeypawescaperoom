A WebQuest · Short Story Unit

The Monkey's Paw

"It had a spell put on it by an old fakir… to show that fate ruled people's lives."W. W. Jacobs, 1902

Introduction

A dried-up monkey's paw arrives at a quiet house on a stormy night. It grants three wishes to three different people. Every wish comes true, but never the way anyone hoped.

In this WebQuest you will read W. W. Jacobs's famous horror story, research the man who wrote it, hunt for clues the author planted, and decide for yourself: is it ever safe to wish against fate?

The Task

Your team of three will become Paw Investigators. Each investigator takes one Mission Card below. When all three missions are done, your team will combine your findings into one final product: a Case File (poster, slideshow, or Google Doc) that answers the question "Who is to blame for what happened to the White family?"

Before you start: read or listen to the full story using the links in Resources. Then play the Paw Trivia game to check your understanding.

The Process 3 mission cards

Read your card, visit the linked resources, and complete the output. Share with your team when you finish.

I

The Historian

Find out about the author and the world of the story.

  • Read about W. W. Jacobs and his Britannica entry. What was his day job? When was he born?
  • Sergeant-Major Morris served in India. Skim British Raj. Why would a British soldier be there in 1902?
  • Read the story's background.

Output: 5 facts about the author and his time, plus 1 sentence on how the setting adds to the creepy mood.

II

The Detective

Hunt for foreshadowing: clues that warn the reader something bad is coming.

  • Reread Part I of the story.
  • Find at least 4 clues (hint: Morris's face, the fire, the face in the flames, Herbert's jokes).
  • For each clue, explain what it hints at later in the story.

Output: a 4-row clue chart: Quote → What it foreshadows → Page/Part.

III

The Wish Lawyer

The paw twists every wish. Your job: beat it.

  • Look closely at how each of the three wishes went wrong.
  • Rewrite Mr. White's first wish so the paw cannot twist it. Then trade with a teammate and try to find a loophole in each other's wish.
  • Watch one film version and note one thing it changed from the text.

Output: your "loophole-proof" wish, the loophole your partner found, and 2 sentences comparing the film and the story.

Conclusion

"The Monkey's Paw" has been retold in films, TV shows, and cartoons for more than 120 years because its warning still lands: be careful what you wish for. Think about it one last time. If someone handed you the paw today, would you make a wish? Write your answer in 3–5 sentences and use one detail from the story to explain why.
